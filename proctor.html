Proctor parked

Watching a game window and telling what happened in it turns out to need more than measuring how much the picture moved. Tested against real footage, this was wrong often enough that it is switched off rather than trusted.

/ WHY IT IS OFF

Replayed against four hours of real gameplay it found 65 augment screens in fifteen minutes, where there were two. And a still screen turned out not to mean an idle player: TFT animates on its own, so the board keeps moving whether or not anyone is playing. Both checks are disabled until they are rebuilt on what is on screen rather than how much of it changed.

/ WHAT STILL HOLDS

The capture side works and never touches the game, the video never leaves your machine, and nothing is sent anywhere. Whether the watching is any good is measured at the lab, on a recording, before any of it is believed.

/ SETUP

Pick the TFT window itself rather than the whole screen — less to watch, and nothing else of yours is ever in frame.
